What we cover

Autism, ADHD and closely related conditions

BrightPost focuses on neurodevelopmental differences: the ones that start in childhood and shape how a person learns, moves, communicates or pays attention. These often show up together, so we cover them as a group.

ConditionIn a few wordsWhere to start
Autism (ASD), including what used to be called Asperger'sDifferences in social communication, plus focused interests, routines or sensory differences.Getting a diagnosis · Autism groups
ADHDOngoing patterns of inattention, hyperactivity or impulsivity that started in childhood.Adults · ADHD groups
Learning differences: dyslexia, dyscalculia, dysgraphiaPersistent difficulty with reading, math or writing that isn't explained by teaching or effort.School evaluations · Learning groups
Dyspraxia (developmental coordination disorder)Difficulty planning and coordinating movement, from handwriting to sports.Who can diagnose
Tourette syndrome and tic disordersRepeated movements or sounds (tics) that are hard to hold back.Tourette groups
Sensory processing differencesBeing over- or under-sensitive to sound, touch, light and more. Not a separate diagnosis in the main manuals, but common with autism and ADHD.Sensory resources
Developmental language disorder (DLD)Lasting difficulty learning, understanding and using language, not explained by hearing loss or autism.A speech-language pathologist evaluation · Language resources
Intellectual disabilityDifferences in learning and everyday skills that start in childhood.The Arc and other groups
Twice-exceptional (2e)Gifted children and adults who also have ADHD, autism or a learning difference.2e resources
OCDUnwanted, repeated thoughts and the urge to do things to ease them. Often seen alongside autism, ADHD and tics.OCD resources

What we don't cover

We aren't a general mental-health service. For the conditions below, these places can help you find care:

  • Depression, anxiety on its own, bipolar disorder, psychosis, eating disorders, trauma and PTSD, and substance use: the SAMHSA National Helpline, 1-800-662-4357, free and open 24/7, gives treatment referrals and information (it isn't counseling). FindTreatment.gov lists state-licensed treatment near you.
  • Dementia and brain injury: start with your doctor.
  • Any crisis: call or text 988, or call 911 if someone is in danger.
Not sure where you fit? You don't have to know. Self-identification counts here, and the people in our support list can help you sort it out.

BrightPost is a guide, not a clinic. We aren't licensed psychologists, doctors or lawyers, and nothing on this site is a diagnosis, treatment or medical, psychological or legal advice. We point you to the people and organizations who can help. Always check details with them, since services and phone numbers change.

In danger right now? Call 911. In a mental-health crisis, call or text 988, or chat at chat.988lifeline.org.
