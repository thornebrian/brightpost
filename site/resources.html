Organizations that can help

A starting list of U.S. organizations, grouped by need. We don't endorse any of them or get anything for listing them, and we list a few you may have mixed feelings about, with a note on why. Phone numbers are as shown on each organization's own site; please check before you call.

Autism

Autistic Self Advocacy Network (ASAN)Autistic-led

Run by and for autistic people. Policy work, self-advocacy toolkits and plain-language guides.

Email info@autisticadvocacy.org

Autism Society of America

National helpline and local affiliates for information, referrals and support. Not a crisis line.

1-800-328-8476

Organization for Autism Research (OAR)

Free guidebooks, scholarships for autistic students and job-seeker programs.

(866) 366-9710

Autistic Women & Nonbinary Network (AWN)

Community and resources for autistic women, girls, nonbinary, trans and Two Spirit people.

Autism Speaks

Large advocacy and research-funding group with an Autism Response Team for family information and referrals. Some autistic advocates, including ASAN, criticize its leadership, spending and messaging; it has since dropped the goal of a cure from its mission.

ADHD

CHADD: National Resource Center on ADHD

Evidence-based information, webinars and parent toolkits, funded mainly through the CDC. It doesn't give counseling or refer you to specific professionals.

1-866-200-8098, Mon–Fri 1–5 pm ET, Spanish available

Attention Deficit Disorder Association (ADDA)

Focused on adults with ADHD: online peer support groups, webinars and workplace resources.

Online contact form

Learning differences

Learning Disabilities Association of America (LDA)

Information, state resources and advocacy for learning disabilities and ADHD.

(412) 341-1515

UnderstoodLeadership includes ND people

Nonprofit guides, tools and community for ADHD, dyslexia and other kinds of neurodivergence.

International Dyslexia Association (IDA)

Directory of evaluators, tutors and advocates. IDA doesn't vet the providers listed.

National Center for Learning Disabilities (NCLD)

Policy, research and resources for parents, young adults and educators.

Language

NIDCD: Developmental language disorder

Plain-language facts from the National Institutes of Health, including who evaluates DLD (a speech-language pathologist).

Tourette syndrome and tics

Tourette Association of America

Toolkits for families and schools, a provider directory, chapters and support groups.

888-4-TOURET, or web chat

Sensory processing

STAR Institute

Research, education and a treatment directory for sensory processing differences.

Intake by online form

Intellectual and developmental disabilities

The Arc of the United States

Rights and support for people with intellectual and developmental disabilities and their families, with hundreds of state and local chapters.

Twice-exceptional (2e)

Davidson Institute: 2e resource guide

A free parent guide to twice-exceptionality: identification, school options, IEP and 504 plans, and social-emotional support.

OCD

International OCD Foundation (IOCDF)

Searchable directory of over 2,000 therapists, clinics and support groups.

(617) 973-5801

Families

Family Voices: Family-to-Family Health Information CentersFamily-led

Free help with health coverage and care, run by parents who've been there. In every state.

Parent to Parent USAParent-led

Get matched one-to-one with an experienced parent for support.

School and legal rights

Parent Training and Information Centers

Nearly 100 federally funded centers that help families with special education, at no cost.

Protection & Advocacy agencies (NDRN)

Legal advocacy agencies in every state for disability rights, including special education.

Council of Parent Attorneys and Advocates (COPAA)

Directory of special-education attorneys and advocates. COPAA itself doesn't represent families.

Office for Civil Rights (U.S. Dept. of Education)

Where to file a disability discrimination or Section 504 complaint against a school, usually within 180 days.

Work

Job Accommodation Network (JAN)

Free, confidential advice on workplace accommodations, funded by the U.S. Department of Labor.

(800) 526-7234

Government guides

CDC: Learn the Signs. Act Early.

Milestone checklists, the Milestone Tracker app and Early Intervention contacts by state.

NIMH: Autism and ADHD

Plain-language health information from the National Institute of Mental Health.

Crisis and other help

988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

Free, confidential and open 24/7. Call or text 988, or chat online. In Spanish, press 2 or text AYUDA to 988.

SAMHSA National Helpline

Free, 24/7 treatment referrals and information for mental health and substance use, in English and Spanish. It isn't counseling.

1-800-662-4357

911

When someone is in danger right now. Call if you can; text only where your area supports it.

BrightPost is a guide, not a clinic. We aren't licensed psychologists, doctors or lawyers, and nothing on this site is a diagnosis, treatment or medical, psychological or legal advice. We point you to the people and organizations who can help. Always check details with them, since services and phone numbers change.

In danger right now? Call 911. In a mental-health crisis, call or text 988, or chat at chat.988lifeline.org.
